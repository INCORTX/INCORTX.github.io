Reference 📖 Read on your own Presentation Topics · Part 1

Each session offers 10 topics — your group picks one to research and present next session:

After Session 1 — Digital Signals → present S2
  1. How sound / image / sensor signals become digital
  2. How ADCs work in IoT devices & sensors
  3. Analog vs digital signals in audio gear
  4. Quantization & sound quality (audio bit depth)
  5. How biomedical signals (ECG/EEG) are digitized
  6. Signal energy vs power — how loudness meters (RMS/VU) use it
  7. Sampling vs quantization — the two steps of digitizing
  8. SNR and quantization noise — why more bits = cleaner sound
  9. Non-audio signals — stock prices, sales, daily step counts as discrete-time signals
  10. Dynamic range & clipping — why recording too loud distorts
After Session 2 — Sampling → present S3
  1. Why CDs use 44.1 kHz — the story behind it
  2. Aliasing in video (wheels spinning backward)
  3. Sampling rates in real devices (phones, cameras)
  4. Nyquist & choosing resolution in practice
  5. Anti-aliasing filters in cameras / audio
  6. How DACs turn numbers back into sound — reconstruction & zero-order hold
  7. What oversampling does in audio gear
  8. Why phone audio (8 kHz) sounds duller than music
  9. Clock jitter — how uneven sampling instants degrade a signal
  10. Sampling rates on wearables / GPS — resolution traded against battery
After Session 3 — LTI & Convolution → present S4
  1. How echo / reverb comes from convolution
  2. LTI systems in real life (room acoustics, circuits)
  3. Convolution reverb in music software
  4. What a room / speaker impulse response is
  5. Convolution applications beyond audio
  6. How convolution properties (commutative/associative) let you merge filters in a pipeline
  7. Causality — why real-time systems cannot use future samples
  8. Step response vs impulse response
  9. Systems that are not LTI — clipping amps, auto-gain control
  10. Measuring a real room's impulse response (clap / sine sweep) and using it
After Session 4 — Moving Average → present S5
  1. Moving average smoothing stock / economic data
  2. Filtering sensor noise (temperature, humidity)
  3. Moving average in health data (steps, heart rate)
  4. Smoothing weather data
  5. Trade-off: smoothing vs responsiveness
  6. Why filters distort signal / image edges — edge effects & padding (zero/same/full)
  7. Weighted vs simple moving average
  8. Exponential moving average (EMA) for streaming data
  9. The 7-day average in daily case / sales charts — why 7
  10. Median filter vs moving average when the data has spikes
After Session 5 — Sinusoid & Frequency → present S6
  1. Music & notes viewed as frequency
  2. How equalizers in music apps work
  3. Why instruments sound different (harmonics)
  4. Sound synthesis from sinusoids
  5. Frequency & human hearing (20Hz–20kHz)
  6. Why piano & violin differ on the same note — harmonics & Fourier series
  7. What is phase, and does it affect what we hear?
  8. Beat frequency — why two close tones "throb"
  9. 50/60 Hz mains hum in ECG and microphones — source and removal
  10. How a tuner app measures the pitch of a note

Reference 📖 Read on your own Presentation Topics · Part 2

Topics for the second half — the final round is researched after Session 7 and presented in Session 8:

After Session 6 — DFT → present S7
  1. What spectrum analyzers in real apps show
  2. Machine vibration analysis
  3. DFT in compression (JPEG / MP3 links)
  4. Detecting abnormal frequencies in signals
  5. DFT in speech analysis
  6. DFT properties (linearity, time-shift) & removing DC offset
  7. Spectral leakage — why non-integer-period signals smear
  8. Windowing (Hann/Hamming) to reduce leakage
  9. Why the DFT of a real signal is symmetric — and how to exploit it
  10. Frequency resolution vs signal length — when two close tones merge
After Session 7 — FFT & Spectrogram → present S8
  1. How Shazam identifies songs via spectrogram
  2. How voice assistants process speech
  3. Spectrograms for animal / bird sounds
  4. Detecting abnormal sounds (engines, hearts)
  5. How FFT speeds up the DFT — why it matters
  6. Why zero-padding looks finer but adds no real resolution
  7. The time–frequency trade-off in a spectrogram
  8. Why STFT frames overlap
  9. Mel-spectrogram / MFCC — what audio AI models are actually fed
  10. FFT on microcontrollers — compute budget vs real time

Rounds run after Sessions 1–7 and are presented in Sessions 2–8 — Session 8 is the last taught session, so the 5th round closes there. The two project sessions (weeks 12 and 14) are separate and are not make-up rounds.
