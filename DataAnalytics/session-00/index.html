Assignment 1 & Marking

Everything about how the group assignment is set and marked. The twenty topics themselves are in the session 1 notebook, where you pick one and load its data.

Groups of four. One topic per group, first come first served. You keep that dataset for the rest of the course.

01The task

Groups of four. One topic per group, first come first served, no two groups on the same one. You keep this dataset for the rest of the course — it is the one you will walk end to end in session 5.

Take the topic you picked and go the whole way:

real data -> EDA -> preprocessing (Pipeline) -> baseline -> model -> a metric that fits -> limitations

Three requirements, all of them straight out of session 1, and all of them things most groups get wrong:

  1. You must have a baseline for your model to beat. A number with nothing to compare it to means nothing. (Acceptable baselines: always predict the most common class · always predict the mean · a one-line rule from domain knowledge.)
  2. You must use a Pipeline, and train_test_split must come before any transformer is fitted. Never fit a scaler or an imputer on the whole dataset.
  3. You must choose a metric that fits the problem and be able to say why. Reporting accuracy alone on skewed classes is an incomplete answer.

You are not required to get a good result. A group whose model loses to its own baseline can still score full marks, as long as they explain why and say what they would try next.

02Marking — 10 marks

CriterionMarks

EDA — at least two diagnostic charts, each with what it told you and what you did about it1
Preprocessing — done inside a Pipeline · no leakage · you can justify how you handled missing values and outliers2

The mark belongs to the group and everybody gets the same one, Q&A included. Questions go to whoever the asker picks, so everyone has to understand the group's code — you cannot split the work into four pieces and never read the other three.

The three most expensive mistakes

SymptomCost
scaler.fit(X) before train_test_split (or filling NaNs with the mean of the whole set)−2 (Preprocessing)

Reporting 97% accuracy on data that is 97:3 skewed, without mentioning it−2 (Baseline)

03Answering questions — the 3 marks

There is no set number of questions. The instructor closes the question period when it has run its course. The three marks are for how the group holds up, not for a count:

MarksWhat it looks like
3Every question answered on substance · where the group did not know, they said so and named how they would check
2Most questions answered · one gap, or one answer that did not survive the follow-up
1Only surface questions answered · could not explain their own code, or where a number came from
0Could not answer · or described something the code does not actually do
An honest "we did not check that, and here is how we would find out" scores higher than a confident answer that turns out to be wrong. Bluffing is the fastest way to lose these marks.

04Asking questions — marks for the audience

Questions do not come only from the instructor. The other groups ask too, and asking earns marks.

What those marks are for is the question, not the fact that you opened your mouth:

MarksThe questionExample
2Changes what the presenting group would do — points at a real flaw, or asks for a number that matters and is not on the slides"Your scaler is fitted before the split. Does the score change if you move it inside the Pipeline?"
1A genuine clarification that helps the whole room follow"Why the median rather than the mean for the missing ages?"
0Asked to be seen asking — already answered on a slide, or about something that does not affect the result"How long did this take?" · "Why did you pick this topic?"

Limits, so the marks spread around the room:

  • At most 2 marks per person per session
  • At most one scoring question per presenting group — you cannot follow one group around
  • You cannot earn asking marks while your own group is presenting

These marks sit outside the 10 for your own presentation. They count towards participation.

05Presenting

6 minutes of talking, then questions · 6–8 slides · every member speaks

Budget about 3 minutes of questions per group, but there is no fixed number of them — the instructor decides when the questions have run their course.

Suggested structure, one slide each:

  1. The question, and where the data came from (how many rows, how many columns)

2–3. Your two EDA charts, and what they told you

  1. Where the data was dirty, and what you did about it
  2. Baseline score vs model score
  3. The metric you chose and why, plus a confusion matrix
  4. Three limitations

06Questions you can prepare for

The same list works both ways — expect these about your own work, and use them on other groups.

  • What does this line do, and what changes if I delete it?
  • Why did you fill missing values that way? What happens if you use the other method?
  • How many steps are in your Pipeline, and what data is each one fitted on?
  • Where does your baseline number come from? Why that baseline?
  • Which line of code produced the number on slide 5?
  • If we cared more about recall than precision, what would you change?
  • Your baseline and your model are close — what would convince you the model is worth keeping?
  • Which of your three limitations would you fix first, and how?

07What to hand in

  1. Slides (PDF)
  2. Code — a shared Colab link anyone can open, or an .ipynb that runs from the first cell
  3. Data — the file itself, or a link/script that fetches exactly the same data
  4. A short README — how to run it, and who did what

08AI and attribution

You may use AI to help write code, but you must understand every line you hand in — the Q&A is where that gets checked. If you take code or a notebook from anywhere (Kaggle, GitHub, a blog post), say so on your slides and in the README. Not saying so counts as copying.

About Kaggle: several topics below already have ready-made notebooks online. You may read them, but you must cite them, and the baseline and the limitations have to be your own — because almost none of those notebooks have either.
