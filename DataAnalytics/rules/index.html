Rules & Marking

The full rules, in the order a cycle runs. Open the part you need.

1How a session runs›Present last week’s work, watch the demo, then an hour on your topic.

Groups of 3–4, named A–J. Every session has its own list of 12–15 topics and you pick again, first come first served. The list comes out a week ahead — arrive with the data loaded.

TimeWhat happens
60 minEvery group presents last session's work — 5+ minutes, then questions (your share of the hour is announced on the day)
45 minDemo — watch, do not type along
60 minYour hour — your topic, the five steps below
StepBy the end of the hour
0Questionone sentence: what you predict, and what one row is
1EDAa chart cell that has run
2Insightone sentence on what you found
3Issuewhat gets in the way — in the data or in the question
4Solutionwhat you did and why, inside a Pipeline
5Metricwhich metric, why, and what baseline() scored
  • Training a model is a bonus; finish it at home.
  • A model that loses to its baseline scores just as well, if you can say why.
  • Session 1: no presentation hour, so the work block is 120 minutes. You pick a topic in the room.
  • Assignment 6: there is no session 7, so present it at the end of session 6 — 2 minutes and one question on whatever you have — then hand in as usual.
2What is marked›Seven items, each on a five-level scale. The mark belongs to the group.
  • 1Presentingall five steps, each opened on screen from the notebook you handed in · top marks for an insight that changed your next step, a metric argued from your data, or doubt about your own result
  • 2Answering questionsanyone in the group may be asked — about your code and your work
  • 3Every file, on timefour files, correctly named, two days before the next session — all or nothing
  • 4The videoeveryone speaks, clear audio, explains rather than reads, and goes deeper than the live version
  • 5The slidescharts titled, axes labelled, captioned · one concrete example · readable in the video
  • 6The homeworkmodel trained · results beside the baseline · two settings compared · error analysis · three limitations of your own data
  • 7Lengthslides 10+ pages · video 5+ minutes

Points for an item = its full points × the level:

100% Excellent75% Good50% Satisfactory25% Needs work0% Missing

Counted directly instead: every file on time (all or nothing), and the marks the audience earns for asking.

The mark belongs to the group — everyone gets the same, questions included. Whoever is asked must be able to explain any line in the file, not only their own part.

3In the room›Two of you present for 5+ minutes; questions come from the instructor and the room.

When — at the start of the next session, from the notebook you handed in. It is already on the room's machine. No slides: scroll the notebook, and save it scrolled to your first cell.

Who — two representatives. Speaker 1: Question → EDA → Insight. Say who you hand over to. Speaker 2: Issue → Solution → Metric. Both take the questions. Spend most of the time on EDA and Insight. Every member still speaks — in the video.

How long — 5+ minutes, then questions. Rehearse it together with the video.

Questions from the instructor

One short question about your code, to anyone in the group:

  • Explain a line — what does this do, and what breaks without it? why median and not mean? fit or transform?
  • Find it — where do you handle missing values? which line splits train and test? which line produced that number?

The second kind shows who has read their own notebook. An honest "we did not check — here is how we would" beats a confident wrong answer.

Questions from the room

Other groups ask too, and earn participation marks — 3–5 questions per group, first hand up.

The questionEarns
Changes what the group should do next — a flaw, a wrong metric, a missed limitationmost
Makes something clearer for everyonesome
Already answered, or could be asked of any groupnothing

Capped per person per session · one question per presenting group · not while your own group presents.

Questions to practise
  • What does this line do, and what changes if I delete it?
  • Why did you fill missing values that way? What happens with the other method?
  • How many steps are in your Pipeline, and what data is each one fitted on?
  • What did baseline() print, and what does it mean for your problem?
  • Which line of code produced the number on this slide?
  • If recall mattered more than precision, what would you change?
  • Your baseline and your model are close — why keep the model?
  • Which of your three limitations would you fix first, and how?
4Hand-in and file names›Four files, uploaded two days before the next session, named DA_ID_SLOT_Topic.

Two days before the next session (the date is on Google Classroom), upload four files:

FileFormat
Notebook.ipynbColab: File → Download → .ipynb · this is the notebook you present
Video.mp4 or .mov5+ minutes · every member speaks
Slides.pdf or .pptx10+ pages
README.md or .txtwhere the data came from · anything borrowed · where you used AI

At the end of each session, also hand in the notebook as it stands — a record of the hour, not marked.

Files only. Upload each file on its own (Add → File). No links — YouTube, Drive, Google Slides, Colab — no .zip or .rar, and no Drive file you still own. The one link allowed: where the data comes from, written in the README.

File names

DA_ID_SLOT_Topic.ext — ID is your group (GA–GJ), or your 8-digit student ID if you work alone · SLOT is the assignment, R1–R6, or PROP / RES for the project · Topic in English CamelCase.

  • Group B, assignment 2: DA_GB_R2_HousePriceRegression.ipynb · .mp4 · .pdf · .md
  • Working alone: DA_69012345_R2_HousePriceRegression.ipynb
  • Project: DA_GB_PROP_AirQualityForecast.pdf · DA_GB_RES_AirQualityForecast.ipynb

No spaces, no Thai · a resubmission ends in _v2. A late, linked, zipped or wrongly named file counts as not handed in.

5The slide deck›Ten pages or more; pages 3–10 are marked.

10+ pages; pages 3–10 are marked. Pages 3–6 are what you presented in the room; 7–10 go further.

PageWhat goes on it
1title · group letter · members · TOPIC_ID · who presented in the room · who speaks in the video
2the question · what one row is · data source (real link) · rows × columns · licence
3EDA — the chart, and what you found in it
4what is wrong with this data — where your topic's trap showed up
5what you did about it, and why
6the metric and why it fits · what baseline() scored and what that means
7results, with the baseline beside them
8two settings compared — which way the result moved, and why
9where the result is wrong (see below) · three limitations
105–10 lines of your own code, explained line by line

Every chart needs a title, labelled axes and a caption. Text must be readable in the video. More pages are fine when they add something real. If the notebook changed after the session, say what and why on page 2.

Page 8 — two settings

Two values of one thing — max_depth=4 against max_depth=8, 3 clusters against 5, with a column against without it. Not two algorithms, and no tuning. Two rows, plus one sentence on which way the result moved and why — the sentence is what counts.

Page 9 — where the result is wrong
TaskShow
Classificationconfusion matrix · 2–3 rows it got wrong, and what they share
Regressionresidual plot · the 2–3 rows it missed most
Clusteringpoints between clusters · an almost empty cluster · one you cannot describe
Association ruleshigh confidence but lift near 1 · a rule that is true and useless
Autoencoderthe 5 worst reconstructions · the class it reconstructs worst
GANbroken samples · near-identical samples (mode collapse)
Reinforcement learningthe shortest episodes · the state the agent failed in
6AI, sources and common mistakes›AI is allowed if you understand it; cite what you borrow; the mistakes that cost marks.

AI is allowed if you understand every line you hand in — the questions check this. Say where you used it in the README; "AI wrote this part and we read it until we understood it" loses nothing.

Borrowed code or notebooks (Kaggle, GitHub, a blog): cite them on the slides and in the README, or it counts as copying. The baseline and the limitations must be your own.
