Data Analytics

Lab sessions for Data Analytics. Hands-on sessions running alongside the lecture course, from raw files through to models you can defend.

Every session runs the same way: the lecture’s own method on real data, then what that first number hides, and always a baseline you have to beat.

Every session is an assignment, and they are all marked the same way

Group work, three or four people, groups named A–J. One topic per group.

60 minEveryone presents — last session's work
45 minDemo — watch, do not type along
60 minYour hour — your topic, five steps

You present a session late, on purpose. The work you do in the hour is finished at home, handed in two days before the next session, and presented at the start of it — so you get a week to make something worth the time on screen. Session 1 has no presentation hour and a 120-minute work block instead; the last assignment is shown briefly at the end of session 6 itself, since there is no session after it, and handed in like all the others.

The five steps of your hour are also exactly what you present: open with the question, then EDA → Insight → Issue → Solution → Metric. Each one has to be opened on screen from your notebook.

Seven things are marked:

  • 01Presenting, at the start of the next session
  • 02Answering questions — the instructor's about your code, the room's about your work
  • 03Handing in every file on time
  • 04Video — fluent, and it analyses something
  • 05Slides — visualisation worth looking at
  • 06The homework checklist, complete
  • 07Slide count and video length
Four rules that decide marks before anything else is read
  • Everything is a file, uploaded to Google Classroom. No YouTube, Drive, Google Slides or Colab links — .ipynb, .mp4, .pdf/.pptx, README.
  • Homework is due two days before the next session, and it is the same every session: notebook, video, slides, README. That notebook is the one you present.
  • Every member of the group speaks in the video, every assignment. The room is different: your group sends two representatives, and they take the questions.
  • You are not required to get a good result. A model that loses to its own baseline scores just as well, if you can say why.
