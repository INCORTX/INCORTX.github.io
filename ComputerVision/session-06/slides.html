A · Corners

Flat, edge or corner: slide a small window

Put a small window on the photo (click anywhere) and shift it a few pixels in every direction. The map on the right: how much the window's pixels change for each shift — dark = no change.

Flat: no change anywhere. Edge: change only across the edge. Corner: change in every direction — the only kind we can find again exactly.

🇹🇭 กดปุ่ม flat / edge / corner (หาจากภาพจริงโดยโปรแกรม) แล้วคลิกที่อื่นเอง · ชี้แผนที่ขวา: ดำทั้งหมด / แถบเดียว / จุดดำจุดเดียวตรงกลาง

🇬🇧 Press flat / edge / corner (picked from the photo by the program), then click elsewhere. The right map: all dark / one dark band / a single dark spot in the middle.

A · Corners

Gradients in the window make a 2 × 2 matrix

$$M=\sum_{\text{window}} w(x,y)\begin{bmatrix} g_x^2 & g_x g_y \\ g_x g_y & g_y^2 \end{bmatrix}$$
window (slide 5)M
flat[0.00 0.00 / 0.00 0.00]
edge[5.63 −1.12 / −1.12 0.25]
corner[2.06 −0.27 / −0.27 1.81]

measured on the photo: Sobel gradients of brightness 0–1, Gaussian window σ = 1.5 px

Session 3 C: Sobel gives gx and gy at every pixel. Session 3 B: a Gaussian weights the middle of the window most.

Add up gx², gxgy and gy² over the window, weighted by w. Four numbers (two equal) say how the window changes when it moves.

The flat window has almost no gradient: M ≈ 0. The edge has a big gx or gy in one direction only.

🇹🇭 ย้อนคาบ 3 C (Sobel) และ 3 B (Gaussian) · ชี้ตาราง: พื้นเรียบ M ≈ 0 · ขอบมีค่าใหญ่ทิศเดียว · มุมใหญ่ทั้งสองทิศ

🇬🇧 Back to session 3 C (Sobel) and 3 B (Gaussian). The table: flat M ≈ 0, an edge is large in one direction, a corner in both.

A · Corners

Two large eigenvalues mean a corner

The two eigenvalues λ₁ ≥ λ₂ of M measure how strongly the window changes along its steepest direction and across it.

Flat: both ≈ 0. Edge: λ₁ large, λ₂ ≈ 0 (here 5.86 and 0.02). Corner: both large (2.23 and 1.64).

🇹🇭 ชี้จุดสามจุดบนกราฟ (ค่าจริงจากหน้าต่างหน้า 5) · มุม = ทั้งสอง eigenvalue ใหญ่ · ไม่ต้องหา eigenvalue เอง — หน้าถัดไปมีทางลัด

🇬🇧 Point at the three dots (real values from slide 5's windows). A corner has both eigenvalues large. No need to compute eigenvalues — the next slide has a shortcut.

A · Corners

Harris: one number instead of two eigenvalues

$$R=\det M-k\,(\operatorname{trace} M)^2$$

det M = λ₁λ₂ and trace M = λ₁ + λ₂ — no eigenvalues needed. k ≈ 0.04.

R ≫ 0: corner · R < 0: edge · R ≈ 0: flat. Keep the points where R is larger than its neighbours and above a threshold.

🇹🇭 ลาก threshold: ยิ่งต่ำยิ่งได้มุมมาก (1% ของค่าสูงสุด = 518 จุด · 5% = 332) · สลับ R map: มุมสว่าง ขอบมืดกว่าพื้น

🇬🇧 Drag the threshold: lower keeps more (1% of the maximum = 518 corners, 5% = 332). Switch to the R map: corners bright, edges darker than flat.

A · Corners

Try it: corner or edge?

Try itUse k = 0.04.
  1. M = [10 0 / 0 5]. Compute R. Corner, edge or flat?
  2. M = [10 0 / 0 0]. Compute R. Corner, edge or flat?
Answer1. det = 50, trace = 15 → R = 50 − 0.04 · 225 = 50 − 9 = 41 → corner · 2. det = 0, trace = 10 → R = 0 − 0.04 · 100 = −4 → edge.

det [a b / c d] = ad − bc · trace = a + d.

R = det − k · trace².

B · Scale

Zoom in, and a corner becomes an edge

whole photo · λ₁ 0.94, λ₂ 0.44 → corner

close-up, same spot · λ₁ 1.26, λ₂ 0.001 → edge

the same 15 × 15-px window (red) on the same point of the building — the second position found through the matches of slide 1

In the close-up everything is about 2× bigger. The window still covers 15 × 15 pixels, so it now sees only one side of the corner: an edge.

Harris uses one window size. To find the same point in both photos, the window must grow with the picture: look at every scale.

🇹🇭 ชี้หน้าต่างแดงสองภาพ: จุดเดียวกันของตึก (หาตำแหน่งผ่าน homography ของคู่ match หน้า 1) · ค่า λ วัดจริง: ภาพเต็มเป็นมุม ภาพซูมเป็นขอบ

🇬🇧 Point at the two red windows: the same spot on the building (located through slide 1's matches). Measured λ: a corner in the whole photo, an edge in the close-up.

B · Scale

Scale space: the photo at every blur

Session 3 B: a Gaussian of width σ blurs away detail smaller than about σ. Session 3 D: a pyramid blurs, then halves.

Blur the photo with σ = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16: a stack of the same picture, from fine to coarse — its scale space.

Bricks vanish first, then windows, then whole floors. A feature has a size: the σ where it is clearest.

🇹🇭 ลาก σ 0 → 16: อิฐหายก่อน ตามด้วยหน้าต่าง แล้วทั้งชั้น · ย้อนคาบ 3 B (σ) และ 3 D (pyramid)

🇬🇧 Drag σ from 0 to 16: bricks go first, then windows, then floors. Back to session 3 B (σ) and 3 D (pyramid).

B · Scale

A blob answers most at its own size

Three bright discs, radius 4, 8 and 16 px. At each σ, measure how blob-like the centre is: the Laplacian of the blurred image, times σ² so that sizes compare fairly.

Each disc peaks at its own σ: 2.75, 5.5, 11.25 — close to r ÷ √2 = 2.8, 5.7, 11.3. The peak tells the size of the point.

🇹🇭 ลาก σ ให้ยอดของแต่ละเส้นผ่าน · ย้ำ: ยอดบอกขนาด (σ ≈ r/√2) — นี่คือวิธีที่ SIFT รู้ว่าจุดใหญ่แค่ไหน

🇬🇧 Drag σ through each peak. The peak gives the size (σ ≈ r/√2) — how SIFT knows how big a point is.

B · Scale

SIFT finds blobs across space and scale

150 SIFT points of the whole photo, the strongest at five sizes — circle radius = the point's size

SIFT subtracts neighbouring blurs (difference of Gaussians ≈ the Laplacian of slide 14) and keeps a point that beats all 26 neighbours: 8 around it, 9 one scale up, 9 one scale down.

So each point comes with a position and a size. In the close-up the matched points are 2.06× bigger — the photo is zoomed 2.23×.

🇹🇭 ชี้วงเล็ก (อิฐ ขอบ) กับวงใหญ่ (หน้าต่างทั้งบาน) · 26 เพื่อนบ้าน = 3 × 3 × 3 − 1 · ขนาดจุดในภาพซูมใหญ่กว่า 2.06× ใกล้กับการซูม 2.23×

🇬🇧 Small circles (bricks, edges), large circles (whole windows). 26 neighbours = 3 × 3 × 3 − 1. In the close-up the points are 2.06× bigger, close to the 2.23× zoom.
